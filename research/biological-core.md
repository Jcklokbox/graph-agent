# Biological core

## Motivation and interpretation

The project explores whether a compact agent can acquire useful behavior through learned associations among sensory conditions, actions, predicted sensory consequences, and outcome value. Graph-like predictive relations and graded, bounded activations offer a conceptual bridge to discussions of neural association, temporal learning, inhibition, and distributed value signals.

These are biological inspirations and modeling analogies, not claims that the software reproduces a particular neural circuit or established biological mechanism. The current implementation is a computational prototype in a small synthetic environment, not a biological model validated against neural or behavioral data.

## Directions for inquiry

Research notes may investigate how temporal credit assignment, context-sensitive associations, inhibition, value updating, and generalization relate to biological learning theories. It can be helpful to distinguish: (1) mechanisms implemented in `development/`, (2) hypotheses motivated by biology, and (3) empirical claims requiring external evidence. The theoretical workspace can consider richer biology, multiple scales, or alternatives to graph-based abstractions without waiting for a corresponding software feature.
