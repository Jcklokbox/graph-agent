## Primary-source anchors and translation limits

- Rescorla, *Probability of shock in the presence and absence of CS in fear conditioning* (1968), [doi](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0025984): match the exposure opportunity in the cue and background, not just total outcomes. D-40's cue hazard 1 identifies no asymptotic noisy-OR difference; D-61 uses identical sub-saturating combined hazards with continuing different tonic risks.
- Kamin, *Predictability, surprise, attention, and conditioning* (1969), pp. 276–296, [bibliographic trace](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3045055/): make surprise, not co-occurrence, discriminant. An inhibitory anchor still signed after compound credit is important: otherwise apparent transfer could be relearning the old cue.
- Bouton, *Context, time, and memory retrieval in the interference paradigms of Pavlovian learning* (1993), [doi](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.114.1.80): separate contextual expression from erased memory. A configuration learner can pass a context interaction; that row alone is not evidence of inferred latent episodes.
- Pearce & Hall, *A model for Pavlovian learning: Variations in the effectiveness of conditioned but not of unconditioned stimuli* (1980), [doi](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.87.6.532): unsigned surprise modulates associability. The factor's residual proposals and posterior variance are computational mechanisms, not an established equivalent attentional learning rule.
- Gallistel & Gibbon, *Time, rate, and conditioning* (2000), [doi](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.107.2.289): retain the observation/time risk set. A forward sensory associate can imply a delayed US and not a one-tick US, while D-61's exploratory forward row scores a one-tick transfer. Do not use its null to reject temporal knowledge, or change the registered scoring; a separate horizon-resolved diagnostic would distinguish them.
