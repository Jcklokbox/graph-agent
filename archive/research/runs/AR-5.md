- Rescorla (1968, contingency) / Kamin (1969, blocking): correlate cue with incremental US over context background, not raw co-occurrence. Neutral-home cue-minus-baseline is meaningful only because location and familiarity are explicitly controlled in both arms; alone it is not proof of associative strength.
- Rescorla & Cunningham (1978, sensory preconditioning): match novelty with a reciprocal AB−/YX− crossover; flat Beta prior can no longer create a signed mediated result merely by predicting .5 more often for an unfamiliar configuration. Check B/X direct conditioning and whether a no-mediation configural baseline still passes.
- Rescorla (1971, inhibition): signed inhibition predicts extra B credit only when B receives the compound's error. Y is familiar and Y alone unreinforced, but AY+ can make it a weak excitor. The matched B probe and BX/BY check are necessary; an excitation-only account must be reported as a competing explanation if Y proves excitatory. Do not claim full summation/retardation validation from the original h1 summation alone.
- Bouton (1993, context and extinction) / Gallistel & Gibbon (2000, time/rate): separate return context and elapsed time from omission opportunities. Renewal readouts subtract matched context-only controls and compare cue increments, not tonic drive. The omission-by-history row estimates a difference in drops, not the raw v1 endpoint. Its signs need not coincide if acquisition differs; keep both rather than choosing the better result.
- Pearce–Hall (1980, associability): uncertainty after omissions is about a cue at an opportunity. Per-step phasic silence is not an omission count. The benchmark manipulates opportunities but the learner receives only raw channels; any learned opportunity attribution needs a counterexample (identical cue rates, different contingency).
